# Personal Market Validation & Outreach

## Summary

A personal workspace for testing multiple B2B and B2C product ideas: challenge assumptions, compare candidate ICPs, gather market evidence, recruit participants, conduct conversations, and progress from problem validation (A) to repeated use (B) to actual payment (C). An embedded CRM and controlled outreach execution support experiments; sending more messages is not the objective.

Status: specification draft synthesized from the user's confirmed decisions. The direction and scope are agreed; detailed defaults and edge-case resolutions below are proposed for implementation review, not evidence that the feature exists or permission to contact anyone.

## Scope

- One operator, multiple products; no team permissions, multi-tenant SaaS, external CRM, generic workflow builder, or full sales forecasting.
- Email automation, manual social-channel tasks, and inbound recruitment via a signup/form workflow. No first-release automated social messaging, meeting attendance, recording, or AI-led interviews.
- Start with empty CRM data. No Jarvis migration, automatic data deletion, or reuse of historical sending approvals. Jarvis was sold and is only a historical design example, not an active pilot. A calorie-estimation app is a possible future example, not authorization to launch a campaign.
- Existing Product ideas remain lightweight. Validation starts explicitly, without converting every saved idea into a mandatory structured project.
- No promise of market demand, inbox placement, statistical certainty from small samples, or exactly-once external delivery.

## Behavior

### Product framing and collaborative ICP definition

1. **P01 — Explicit start.** A new workspace contains no imported contacts, active experiments, queued sends, or implicit sender authorization. The user can begin validation from an existing idea or a new brief without changing unrelated ideas or their notes.
2. **P02 — Common workflow.** Every product follows the same framing process regardless of B2B/B2C classification. Product names, industries, landing-page URLs, professions, and preferred channels are user data, not fixed eligibility rules.
3. **P03 — Critical partner.** The assistant asks focused questions about user, buyer, purchasing authority, triggering situation, problem frequency, consequences, alternatives, costs, and exclusions. It distinguishes user statements, observed facts, AI hypotheses, and unresolved disagreements, and actively seeks counterevidence.
4. **P04 — Candidate comparison.** The assistant proposes two or three candidate segments with situation, problem, existing workaround, observable identification signals, exclusions, evidence, uncertainty, and recruitment feasibility. It may return fewer with an explanation when evidence is insufficient; it must not invent support to fill the list.
5. **P05 — Human choice.** The user chooses or revises a segment and product/offer form. AI cannot silently accept an ICP or change it during research. A small real-world sample calibrates whether the identification criteria find the intended people; identity fit alone does not prove pain.
6. **P06 — Versions, not repeated forms.** Approved ICPs and product/offer descriptions retain their versions and decision reasons. Conversations produce editable drafts of the necessary artifacts; the user is not required to fill an extensive CRM setup form. Earlier approvals and experiment interpretations remain associated with their original versions.
7. **P07 — Disagreement is actionable.** AI proposes a bounded experiment to resolve disagreements. The user may proceed with an explicitly recorded weak-evidence hypothesis; that decision does not bypass contact restrictions, spending approval, or sending authorization.

### Evidence and experiment decisions

8. **P08 — Evidence provenance.** Research observations retain their original source link or supplied document, relevant passage, observation time, and what claim they support or challenge. Unavailable, stale, conflicting, or inaccessible information is visible; a summary is not presented as the source's words.
9. **P09 — Market demand is not supply.** Competitor existence, pricing pages, customer claims, reported budgets, actual payment, and repeat use are distinct evidence categories. The system investigates alternatives including manual work and services, and never equates a competitor's price with verified customer revenue.
10. **P10 — Stage order.** A evaluates a real problem and current coping behavior/cost; B evaluates meaningful use at the product's natural frequency; C evaluates actual payment for a stated offer. Pricing and existing spending can be discussed during A. A registration, compliment, meeting, or promise to pay is not automatically a later-stage success.
11. **P11 — Experiment card.** Before execution, the assistant proposes a hypothesis, ICP and offer versions, recruitment approach, primary outcome, evidence requirements, observation period, sample rationale, spend ceiling, and pass/fail/inconclusive criteria. Proposed numbers are suggestions, not universal defaults or user approvals.
12. **P12 — User-approved criteria.** Only the user approves an experiment's criteria and execution scope. AI may prepare drafts and research within separately authorized limits; it cannot activate a recruitment campaign, buy data, or promote a validation stage by interpreting a conversational suggestion as approval.
13. **P13 — Changed criteria.** Changing approved ICP, offer, or outcome criteria creates a revised experiment context. Previously collected observations retain their original context and are not retrospectively relabeled as passing. Active affected outreach must be paused and explicitly reapproved or left on the original experiment, never silently repurposed.
14. **P14 — Honest outcomes.** Reviews show supporting and conflicting evidence, denominators, missing observations, sample limitations, source/channel differences, and acquisition cost when known. No reply is not automatically no demand; delivery, recruitment, offer, and measurement failures remain alternative explanations.
15. **P15 — Human progression.** AI recommends continue, revise, stop, or gather more evidence. The user records the final outcome and decides whether to begin the next stage. An inconclusive result stays inconclusive unless the user explicitly changes the judgment with a reason; observations remain unchanged.
16. **P16 — Use and payment records.** B and C accept dated, sourced manual observations or imported events identifying the participant/cohort, action, product version, and applicable period. Payment evidence distinguishes charged, refunded, trial, and promised amounts, with currency. First release does not require an analytics SDK, checkout, or payment processor integration.

### Source planning and recruitment

17. **P17 — Source plan.** For each selected segment, the assistant proposes concrete sources, why they contain suitable people, obtainable fields, access method, freshness, limitations, likely cost, and a bounded sample plan. Discovery, enrichment, contact verification, and permission to contact are separate operations.
18. **P18 — Free first, bounded paid use.** Paid sources, subscriptions, and higher-volume runs require explicit approval of provider, purpose, scope, and spending cap. The system does not buy or renew subscriptions. It stops when the approved cap would be exceeded or charges cannot be bounded, including ambiguous already-started paid requests.
19. **P19 — Prove the source.** The user reviews actual sample results before expansion. The source plan records quality, duplicates, missing routes, rejection reasons, and observed cost. Researching how another tool sources data is not proof that its method is accessible or permitted here.
20. **P20 — Authorized access.** Login barriers, unavailable APIs, platform restrictions, and permission failures produce visible limitations or manual tasks, not invented data or an automatic switch to an unauthorized scraping method. Public availability is not blanket permission to collect or contact.
21. **P21 — Two recruitment paths.** Named outbound prospects and voluntary inbound respondents can join the same experiment. Public recruitment has an attributable invitation/form and records its purpose and submission source. Anonymous visitors stay aggregate observations, not guessed CRM contacts.
22. **P22 — Inbound is not blanket consent.** A signup retains the permission wording/scope and submission time when available. A form submission is not verified ownership of someone else's email and does not automatically enroll that address in a sequence. Imports with missing consent or provenance remain flagged for review.
23. **P23 — Reproducible research runs.** Research runs expose scope, progress, partial results, cost where known, errors, and cancellation. Retry does not discard already reviewed results or silently repeat paid operations. External text is evidence, never authority to alter prompts, approvals, budgets, or recipients.

### CRM and lead review

24. **P24 — Shared identity, scoped relationship.** A person has shared contact/history information; fit, review, experiment participation, and outcomes belong to a particular product/experiment. Companies are optional for consumers. Neither company-wide demand nor a person's pain is inferred from a job title alone.
25. **P25 — Actionable review.** Each lead shows who they are, why they might fit, sources, unknowns, contact routes, prior contact history, restrictions, and the proposed next action. The user can accept, reject, or request more evidence, individually or in a clearly scoped bulk action, with reasons.
26. **P26 — Three independent gates.** ICP acceptance, route usability, and sending authorization are separate. Accepting a lead does not send a message or approve every channel. Correcting a recipient address or account requires approval of that changed route before use.
27. **P27 — Duplicates and merges.** The CRM flags suspected duplicates without merging on name alone. Confirmed identity merges preserve all provenance and history, expose conflicting fields, and apply the stricter contact restriction. A duplicate must not acquire a second concurrent cold-outreach sequence by being added to another product.
28. **P28 — Global contact safety.** Before enrollment, show active outreach, past contact, and suppression across products. First-release default is at most one active cold-outreach enrollment per known person; uncertain identity/address collisions block sending pending review. Further experiments never erase earlier refusals.
29. **P29 — Independent status dimensions.** Fit, lead review, enrollment progress, individual delivery outcomes, conversation handling, and validation findings remain separately visible. A bounce is not an ICP rejection; interest is not payment; opening a social profile is not a sent message.
30. **P30 — Contact restrictions.** Unsubscribe and an explicit request not to be contacted block future outreach to the known person across products by default. A narrower documented permission can be reviewed by the user; AI cannot lift a restriction. Hard bounces block the affected route and do not automatically authorize switching channels. Rejection of a particular offer ends that enrollment, but is distinguishable from a global opt-out.

### Sequence approval and personalized generation

31. **P31 — Sequence contents.** A sequence defines its experiment goal, sender, eligible approved participants/routes, ordered steps, channel, approved claims/links, editable generation prompt, timing, maximum touches, exit conditions, and execution limits. First release supports bounded linear steps and stop conditions, not an arbitrary branching workflow language.
32. **P32 — Preview before approval.** The user can inspect representative generated messages against real lead evidence, revise the prompt, and approve the sequence rules and an explicit batch of reviewed recipients. No preview email is sent to a prospect. Approved rules permit personalized messages without per-message approval; newly discovered leads do not inherit batch approval.
33. **P33 — Frozen versions.** A batch pins the approved sequence, prompt, offer, sender, and route versions. Editing a prompt creates a new version for new batches. Existing batches continue on their pinned version unless explicitly paused and transferred to a newly approved version for unsent steps; completed messages are never rewritten.
34. **P34 — Generation limits.** The assistant personalizes only using permitted, sourced information and approved product claims. It must not invent experiences, prior acquaintance, endorsements, pain, or facts about the recipient. Unsubstantiated or invalid output is held for review, not sent because a model labeled it safe. Missing enrichment can produce neutral wording rather than fabricated specificity.
35. **P35 — Prompt is not permission.** Prompt edits cannot expand recipients, channels, number of messages, spend, or sending rights, disable stop conditions, expose unrelated data, or replace the approved sender. Such changes need separate explicit authorization.
36. **P36 — Message traceability.** The actual final message, generation prompt version, evidence references, sender, recipient, approval context, and execution outcome are retained. Retries after a confirmed pre-send failure use the same frozen message unless it is explicitly replaced and reviewed under the applicable rules.
37. **P37 — Manual social execution.** Social steps produce drafts and actionable profile links. The user records completed, skipped, or blocked with an observed time and note. Opening/copying a draft or sending a connection invitation does not record a message as sent. Unsupported automatic sending is labeled unavailable, not promised.

### Execution, pause, and stop

38. **P38 — Explicit activation.** Sending remains disabled until an authorized sender is selected, a batch is approved, the sending policy is configured, and the user activates it. Daily caps, minimum gaps, timezone/window rules, and follow-up intervals are displayed as actually enforced; unknown recipient timezones use a disclosed user-approved fallback or hold.
39. **P39 — Durable progress.** Refreshes, restarts, repeated clicks, and multiple open windows do not create duplicate enrollments or automatically reset delivery history. A process being down delays work; the UI does not claim unattended execution while no runner is available.
40. **P40 — Observable delivery states.** Queued, preparing, dispatching, provider-accepted, delivered-if-confirmed, failed-before-send, uncertain, and cancelled are distinguishable. Provider acceptance does not prove recipient delivery. Ambiguous send results remain uncertain; no automatic resend is allowed just because a timeout or lease expiry occurred.
41. **P41 — Pause scopes.** The user can pause one person's enrollment, a batch, or all outreach. After a pause is acknowledged, no additional dispatch is authorized in that scope. Previously authorized/in-flight requests are shown explicitly and are cancelled where possible, but may still be accepted externally; the system never claims to recall them.
42. **P42 — Resume is deliberate.** Resume requires an explicit action and rechecks restrictions, replies, route/sender validity, approval versions, expired experiments, and limits. Resuming a parent scope does not clear an individual reply pause or opt-out. Overdue steps are rescheduled at normal pacing rather than burst-sent; completed or cancelled steps never recur.
43. **P43 — End versus pause.** Ending an enrollment or batch cancels remaining steps and is not resumable. Starting another enrollment requires new approval and the normal contact-history checks. History remains available; a global emergency pause is a reversible stop, not deletion.
44. **P44 — Automatic holds.** Inbound replies pause the person's cold outreach before AI interpretation. Suspected auto-replies, ambiguous matches, uncertain sends, stale/unavailable reply sync, revoked sender access, or invalid approvals cause a visible hold rather than optimistic continuation. Confirmed bounce/abuse thresholds pause the sender under a configured policy.
45. **P45 — Safe timing and concurrency.** Limits include initial, follow-up, and user-confirmed reply sends made through this workspace. Every dispatch rechecks current stop/restriction state. Replies observed before dispatch authorization prevent it; a reply not yet observable at the provider can race with an already authorized message, and that limitation is disclosed.
46. **P46 — Reconciliation.** The user can inspect and resolve uncertain delivery using provider evidence. Not finding a message in one search is not proof of failure. A second attempt requires a documented resolution and explicit authorization, not a generic retry button that risks duplicate contact.
47. **P47 — Stop receipt.** A stop action shows its persisted result, affected scope, and any already in-flight attempts. If the server cannot confirm the stop, the UI says so and does not display success. Sending remains held when the system cannot read its authoritative approval/stop state.

### Conversations, interviews, and learning

48. **P48 — Human-controlled replies.** AI classifies incoming messages, extracts tentative findings, and drafts replies. The user reviews the exact destination and content before sending every reply. A changed draft or recipient invalidates that approval. Resuming cold outreach is a separate decision, not a side effect of replying.
49. **P49 — Original words and interpretation.** Findings retain the original passage and conversation/source reference. AI inference and user-confirmed interpretation are separately labeled. Interest, objections, alternatives, willingness to try, usage, and payment are distinct; quoted participant instructions cannot authorize system actions.
50. **P50 — Interview support.** The assistant proposes non-leading questions about recent actual behavior. The user may add notes or transcripts obtained with appropriate permission; partial notes remain partial and are not expanded into invented quotations. No automatic meeting attendance, recording, or autonomous interviewing is included.
51. **P51 — Conversation association.** Replies are associated using available thread, message, and contact evidence. Ambiguous replies are surfaced for review and do not attach private unrelated mail to a product. New-thread replies from known contacts can be reviewed and associated without continuing their cold sequence meanwhile.
52. **P52 — Useful operational view.** The workspace shows pending human decisions, due manual steps, reply drafts, blocked/uncertain execution, active experiments, and next actions. Activity counts distinguish people, attempts, accepted messages, replies, and evidence-backed outcomes; manual activity is labeled as user-reported.
53. **P53 — Useful review.** Experiment summaries group results by pinned ICP/offer/channel versions, show denominators and observation periods, and retain negative findings. Small samples are described as directional evidence, not statistically established product-market fit. Opening an email or clicking a link alone is not validation.

### Failure handling, privacy, and lifecycle

54. **P54 — Empty, unavailable, and partial.** Empty lists explain the next action; failed searches, denied permissions, and partial runs show their actual outcome rather than an empty-success state. Users can stop research without losing reviewed results. Network failures preserve edits where possible and distinguish unsaved local content from confirmed server state.
55. **P55 — Concurrent edits.** If another tab changes an artifact after it was loaded, saving or approving stale content yields a conflict with a chance to inspect the latest version. Bulk approvals report the exact accepted set and exclusions; they never approve rows added after the user reviewed the selection.
56. **P56 — Accessible controls.** Approval, stop, resume, and review controls work with keyboard and on mobile; states do not depend on color alone. Pending actions, errors, and focus changes are accessible. Dangerous actions name their scope without burying emergency pause behind a confirmation dialog.
57. **P57 — Minimal private data.** Recruitment gathers only information needed for the approved purpose. Calorie-app research must not infer medical conditions or collect sensitive health details by default. The user can inspect retained data; external enrichment/model calls disclose which data is shared. Secrets and unrelated inbox contents never appear in exports or generated messages.
58. **P58 — Controlled retention and removal.** Archive retains history and stops active work; it is not deletion. Explicit removal of personal content requires confirmation and stops future activity. A minimal restriction record may be retained where appropriate to honor opt-outs, with that retention disclosed. Operational history is append-only during ordinary work, not a promise to retain personal data forever.
59. **P59 — Export and restore.** The user can export products, experiments, contacts, evidence, messages, approvals, restrictions, and events with stable references, excluding credentials. A restore is verified and starts paused; it does not resume old queues or treat missing delivery records as proof that nothing was sent.
60. **P60 — Legacy isolation.** The new CRM neither reads old Jarvis lead/approval files nor starts its old runners. Existing legacy services must be explicitly dealt with before live rollout, especially if a sender is shared. This specification does not itself stop, delete, migrate, or authorize use of the sold project's data.
61. **P61 — Account changes.** Disconnecting or changing a sender pauses affected work. Approval for one account is not permission for another. Other Google integrations remain independent, and reading ordinary personal mail is not a requirement to expose it to market research.
62. **P62 — Human control survives automation.** Research/generation can produce proposals, but only explicit user operations can approve experiment criteria, paid scope, leads, sending rules/batches, reply sends, and stage progression. The audit view identifies the human decision versus AI recommendation and the versions involved.

## Open questions before live rollout

- First real product, target region/audience, sender account, and experiment criteria have not been selected or approved. No campaign is authorized by this document.
- Actual paid-source cap, email cap, pacing, follow-up intervals, sending window, reply-sync freshness threshold, and bounce/abuse thresholds require an explicit operational policy. Legacy Jarvis constants are not defaults.
- The user must choose an acceptable inbound form provider/deployment and data-sharing policy; first release may connect an external form and import submissions rather than host a public service.
- Retention duration, opt-out scope wording, applicable regional contact rules, and model/provider data-sharing settings must be resolved before handling real participants. These are rollout gates, not assumed legal conclusions.
- UI layout/mock review and explicit disposition of any legacy senders remain pending; permission to write this specification is not permission to alter those services.
